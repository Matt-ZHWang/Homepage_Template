01 / Front room

About me

Zihao (Matthew) Wang he / they

I'm Zihao (Matthew) Wang (pronoun: he/they), a Ph.D. student at the Department of Astronomy, School of Physics & Kavli Institute for Astronomy and Astrophysics (KIAA), Peking University, supervised by Prof. Kohei Inayoshi.

Previously, I was a visiting intern student at MIT. Before moving to PKU, I earned my Bachelor's degree from the School of Astronomy & Space Science, Nanjing University.

I study the formation of the first stars, galaxies, and supermassive black holes, and how their early histories become visible in the era of James Webb Space Telescope (JWST).

My current focus is the formation and evolution of high-redshift AGNs and galaxies. To study this, I combine numerical simulations, analytical models, and machine learning, and work through a growing set of linked questions: (i) what drives the rapid growth of BH and the connection to the newly-discovered population, the Little Red Dots (LRDs); (ii) what drives the rapid growth of high-z galaxies and how the BH reshape the host; (iii) Their observational imprints and diagnostics in the era of JWST.

This site is arranged like a small house: the home introduces me, the working rooms hold research projects, the reading table keeps selected papers, and the garden opens into cinema, music, essays, photographs, and notes.
